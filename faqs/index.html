Questions before you begin

The Foundation is intentionally bounded. These answers make the fit, deliverables, price, responsibilities, and clean no-fit stop explicit.

Who is this actually for?

One small business or operating department of roughly 5–20 people with a sponsor who can provide access, make time for the relevant people, and support a small next-step decision after the handoff.

What am I actually paying for?

A fixed, bounded discovery-and-planning engagement: sponsor intake, one six-hour onsite discovery day, synthesis within five business days, a remote leadership readout, and editable decision materials. You are paying for a structured view of the work and one next-step decision—not a finished AI system.

Why not just start with ChatGPT ourselves?

You can, and the Field Guide gives you a place to start. The Foundation is designed for a team that wants to develop a shared view of the work, ownership, risk, evidence, and competing ideas before considering a team-level experiment. It is not a claim that outside help is always necessary.

What happens during the six-hour discovery day?

I meet the relevant people, learn how work moves today, map uneven AI use and repeated friction, identify boundaries, and surface possible responses. The day is for observation and decision support; it is not a build day, training day, or security assessment.

How much time will you need from our team?

The proposal defines the exact access and schedule. The published boundary includes sponsor intake, one six-hour onsite discovery day with the people closest to the work, and one remote leadership readout. It is designed for a focused team, not an organization-wide program.

What information will you need to see?

Only what is necessary to understand the work and make a responsible decision. The proposal sets the access and confidentiality terms. Do not share information that requires specialist review or is unsuitable for the conversation; those questions are flagged, not handled as legal, privacy, security, or compliance advice.

What do we receive?

A team-and-role map, AI-use baseline, potential AI Champion map, ranked opportunity backlog, starter responsible-use guardrails, one recommended 90-day experiment, leadership brief, and editable handoff materials.

What if we discover AI is not the answer?

That is a valid outcome of discovery. A pain point is not automatically an AI or automation opportunity. The work can identify a process, ownership, policy, training, or non-AI change as the more responsible next decision.

Does every recommendation need to use AI?

No. I compare process changes, templates, deterministic automation, conventional software, and AI-enabled experiments. I may use AI to assist discovery, prototyping, or a separately scoped build without making it a required production dependency. If later implementation is justified, its model usage, token costs, data boundaries, output variability, and operating ownership must be evaluated in a separate proposal.

Is this meant to replace people?

No. My principle is that AI should support human judgment, agency, and ownership. The Foundation can identify where a process, tool, or bounded AI experiment may support the team and which work should remain human-owned. It is not a staff-reduction program, and a headcount-reduction goal is a clean no-fit. This does not guarantee job protection or workforce outcomes.

Who owns the work after the handoff?

Your sponsor and team do. The materials are designed for the team to continue without ongoing dependence on me. Whether to run, pause, revise, or decline a proposed experiment remains the sponsor's decision.

Does the Foundation include implementation?

No. Production implementation, custom software, infrastructure, training, maintenance, managed support, and specialist assurance are outside the Foundation. If later work is justified, it needs a separate evaluation and proposal.

Is this a security, legal, privacy, or compliance assessment?

No. The Foundation includes starter responsible-use guardrails and flags questions for qualified specialists; it is not an audit or assurance service.

What happens if the Foundation is not a fit?

The process stops cleanly. It is not a fit when the immediate need is a production application, organization-wide transformation, staff-reduction plan, specialist assessment, or a predetermined implementation decision.

What happens after I send the request?

The request begins a fit conversation—not free implementation. We use the five prompts to understand the team, the improvement sought, approximate size, current AI situation, and preferred reply method. If there is a plausible fit, the next decision is whether a one-page proposal is useful; if not, we close the loop.
