The situation

When the Foundation may fit

The engagement

Listen, map, rank, choose, and hand off

I learn how the team is organized, establish a current AI-use baseline, identify potential AI Champions, and surface repeated pain points.

  1. Listen

    Learn how the team is organized and where repeated pain points create delay, rework, risk, frustration, or avoidable cost.

  2. Map

    Establish the current AI-use baseline and identify potential AI Champions—department-aware employees interested in helping discover, evaluate, and eventually support useful practices, not a certification or formal title.

  3. Rank

    Together, rank opportunities by value, feasibility, risk, and readiness. A pain point is not automatically an AI or automation opportunity.

  4. Choose

    Select one bounded 90-day experiment as the recommended next step.

  5. Hand off

    Receive editable materials designed for your team to continue without ongoing dependence on me.

What you are buying

What an engagement looks like

A fixed, bounded discovery-and-planning engagement—not an implementation project. It is designed to organize shared evidence and support a sponsor and team as they decide whether a 90-day AI experiment is worth pursuing.

  1. 01

    Sponsor intake

    Clarify the team, the decision to make, the available access, and the questions that would make the work a no-fit.

  2. 02

    One six-hour discovery day

    Observe the work with the relevant people, map current AI use, and surface repeated friction without assuming AI is the answer.

  3. 03

    Synthesis and ranking

    Rank possible responses by value, feasibility, risk, and readiness, then identify one bounded next-step decision.

  4. 04

    Leadership readout and handoff

    Review the evidence, recommended experiment, open specialist questions, and editable materials the team can own.

Illustrative composite examples—not clients, case studies, or outcome evidence.

The service team that keeps rebuilding the same answer

Starting situation: A customer-facing team answers recurring questions from email, past documents, and one experienced person's memory. Responses vary, review takes time, and nobody agrees which problem to address first.

Decision to make: Decide whether a shared drafting or knowledge-retrieval practice is worth a bounded experiment before anyone buys or builds a system.

Foundation focus: Map the request-to-response workflow, identify data and quality boundaries, rank the options, and hand off one experiment brief if the evidence supports it.

Clean no-fit: Not a fit if the immediate need is a production knowledge base, CRM integration, customer-data assessment, or custom software.

The operations team with scattered AI experiments

Starting situation: Several people use different AI tools for notes, drafts, and spreadsheets while others avoid them. The sponsor cannot see the useful practices, the risky ones, or the work that should remain unchanged.

Decision to make: Decide which current practice deserves a shared test, what guardrails are needed, and whether there is a potential AI Champion to help the team evaluate it.

Foundation focus: Establish an AI-use baseline, map team roles, surface boundaries, rank opportunities, and recommend one team-owned 90-day experiment only when it is plausible.

Clean no-fit: Not a fit if the team needs an enterprise AI rollout, formal training program, compliance approval, or managed support immediately.

The sponsor deciding whether AI belongs in the next quarter

Starting situation: A sponsor sees recurring administrative friction and pressure to ‘do something with AI,’ but has not defined the work, the decision owner, the sensitive information boundary, or a useful measure of progress.

Decision to make: Decide whether there is one safe, owned problem worth testing next quarter—or whether the better answer is to improve the process without AI.

Foundation focus: Frame the work, identify the evidence and constraints, compare AI and non-AI responses, and create a leadership brief that makes the next decision explicit.

Clean no-fit: Not a fit if the sponsor already needs a vendor selection, staff-reduction plan, security review, or guaranteed savings case.

Before a fit conversation

21 questions before a fit conversation

Use these questions to decide whether the immediate need is a bounded discovery engagement, a different kind of help, or no engagement at all.

The work
  1. Which repeated task is creating delay, rework, risk, frustration, or avoidable cost?
  2. Who touches that work today, and where does it change hands?
  3. What evidence shows that this is worth examining now?
The desired change
  1. What should be easier, clearer, safer, or more consistent if this work improves?
  2. What must remain human-owned or unchanged?
  3. What would make a proposed experiment not worth continuing?
Current AI and data boundaries
  1. How is AI already being used, avoided, or discussed across the team?
  2. What information is sensitive, regulated, confidential, or otherwise unsuitable for a casual test?
  3. Which legal, privacy, security, or compliance questions need a qualified specialist rather than this engagement?
Ownership and decision rights
  1. Who can sponsor the work and make time for the people closest to it?
  2. Who would own a small experiment after the handoff?
  3. Whose agreement or approval would be needed before anything changes?
Evidence and readiness
  1. What facts, examples, or observations would make a next step credible?
  2. Can the team describe a small, reversible experiment rather than a broad transformation?
  3. Is there enough access to map the work without exposing information that should not be shared?
Alternatives and constraints
  1. What has the team already tried, and what did it reveal?
  2. Could a process, policy, training, or non-AI change address the problem better?
  3. What time, tool, budget, or operational constraints would shape a responsible next step?
Fit and next decision
  1. Is the immediate need discovery and decision support, rather than production implementation or specialist assurance?
  2. Would a leadership readout and editable handoff help the sponsor decide what follows?
  3. If the answer is no, what would need to be true before a Foundation engagement could become plausible?

This is a modern consultative synthesis—not a proprietary script or a promise that every answer leads to an engagement.

Automation implementation, custom systems, infrastructure, training, maintenance, and support are not included. If later work is justified, it is evaluated and proposed separately.

Before you begin

Questions before you begin

Who is this actually for?

One small business or operating department of roughly 5–20 people with a sponsor who can provide access, make time for the relevant people, and support a small next-step decision after the handoff.

What am I actually paying for?

A fixed, bounded discovery-and-planning engagement: sponsor intake, one six-hour onsite discovery day, synthesis within five business days, a remote leadership readout, and editable decision materials. You are paying for a structured view of the work and one next-step decision—not a finished AI system.

Why not just start with ChatGPT ourselves?

You can, and the Field Guide gives you a place to start. The Foundation is designed for a team that wants to develop a shared view of the work, ownership, risk, evidence, and competing ideas before considering a team-level experiment. It is not a claim that outside help is always necessary.
