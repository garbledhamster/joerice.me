Run a lightweight AI enablement discovery

Learn what is actually happening in one team's work before choosing tools, policies, or projects.

Reviewed 2026-08-01 · review again by 2026-11-01

What this helps decide

Use this guide to decide whether one repeated work problem deserves deeper attention. The output is a short evidence record, not a promise that AI is the answer.

Before you start

  • Choose one team and one recurring workflow.
  • Name a sponsor who can provide access and make a later decision.
  • Invite three to five people who see different parts of the work.
  • Keep real customer, employee, financial, health, legal, and security-sensitive data out of shared AI tools.
  • Reserve 60 to 90 minutes for interviews and 30 minutes to combine the notes.

The procedure

  1. Write the practical outcome the team is trying to create. Avoid starting with a product name or AI feature.
  2. Ask each person to walk through the last real instance of the work from trigger to finish.
  3. Record handoffs, repeated decisions, waiting, rework, exceptions, and places where context gets lost.
  4. Separate observed facts from interpretations. A fact might be that three files are re-entered; an interpretation is that automation will fix it.
  5. Ask what has already been tried, what made it difficult, and what must remain under human judgment.
  6. Mark any data, legal, security, privacy, or compliance boundary that needs a specialist before experimentation.
  7. Finish with one problem statement that names the actor, situation, friction, and evidence still needed.

Evidence standard

A useful discovery note cites recent examples, names who experiences the friction, distinguishes frequency from severity, and records disagreement. One vivid complaint is a lead to investigate, not proof of a pattern.

Common failure modes

  • Asking what people want AI to do instead of observing the work.
  • Treating the loudest participant as the whole team.
  • Combining several workflows into one vague transformation problem.
  • Recording sensitive examples in a tool that has not been approved for them.
  • Jumping from pain point to software before confirming ownership and constraints.

DIY fit

This is a reasonable DIY exercise when the workflow is bounded, the sponsor can reach the people involved, decisions are reversible, and the team can describe examples without exposing sensitive data.

When to bring in facilitation

Bring in neutral facilitation when departments disagree about the problem, people do not feel safe describing the work, the workflow crosses several owners, the data boundary is unclear, or the next decision carries material operational or reputational risk.
